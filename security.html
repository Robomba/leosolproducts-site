SetCheck — Security Policy

Last updated: 15 August 2026 · Contact: rosinkai888@gmail.com

Reporting a vulnerability

Email rosinkai888@gmail.com with "SECURITY" in the subject. Please include:

  • what you found and where (component, version, URL),
  • how to reproduce it,
  • what an attacker could achieve.

You'll get an acknowledgment within 5 business days and an assessment within 14 days.

SetCheck is maintained by one person, so please allow reasonable time for a fix.

Coordinated disclosure

We ask for 90 days before public disclosure, or until a fix ships — whichever comes first. If a

fix is going to take longer, we'll say so and agree a date with you. If a vulnerability is being

actively exploited, we'll move faster and say so publicly.

Safe harbour

We will not pursue legal action for good-faith security research that:

  • respects user privacy and does not access, modify, or destroy other people's data,
  • does not degrade the service (no DoS, no spam, no social engineering of users or third parties),
  • stays within systems we control (our website and the software itself — not users' storage buckets,

which belong to them and are out of scope),

  • gives us reasonable time to fix before disclosure.

We don't currently run a paid bug bounty. Credit in the acknowledgments is offered if you want it.

Scope

In scope: the SetCheck application, the packaging/encryption engine, the /open recipient page,

and leosolproducts.com pages that serve them.

Out of scope: users' own cloud storage accounts and credentials, third-party services, physical

attacks, and issues requiring a fully compromised local machine.

Design notes for reviewers

  • Content is encrypted client-side with AES-256-GCM, chunked, each chunk authenticated with a

final-chunk flag in the AAD (truncation/extension detection).

  • Link secrets are 128-bit random; PIN-protected deliveries derive keys with **PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256,

310,000 iterations**.

  • Recipient-locked deliveries use X25519 ECDH with HKDF-SHA256 key wrapping.
  • The decryption key travels in the URL fragment and is never sent to a server.
  • No key escrow. We cannot decrypt user content; that is intentional.
  • Encrypted packages and their metadata sidecar live in the user's own storage.

Known and accepted: the metadata sidecar (delivery ID, timestamps, counters, PIN-exists flag, and the

user-typed sender/project names) is stored unencrypted so links can expire and be revoked. Users

are warned not to put sensitive information in those fields.
